Card 1 of 6 · AI toolkit

Obsidian: a second brain made of linked notes

A free note app that keeps every note as a plain text file on your own computer, and lets you link notes to each other.

How it works

Note
One Markdown file (.md): plain text with simple marks, like # for a heading.
Vault
The folder on your computer that holds all your notes.
Link
Type [[note name]] to connect two notes.
Graph view
A map of all your notes and the links between them.

The Zettelkasten method

Sociologist Niklas Luhmann kept about 90,000 paper notes. Each had a number, and he linked notes with these numbers.

1

One idea per note

Keep each note small.

2

Use your own words

Do not copy and paste.

3

Link each note

Connect it to one or more other notes.

A digital firm

  • Key business relationships are digitally enabled.
  • Core business processes run over digital networks.
  • Key assets are managed digitally.

Result: more flexibility

Time shifting: business runs 24/7.

Space shifting: work happens anywhere in the world.

Example: Supercell

A European mobile game company that makes and sells its games entirely digitally.

  • Its assets (code, designs) are digital.
  • Cloud tools give access anytime, anywhere.
  • Games run 24/7 for players worldwide.
  • Flat, agile structure.

Class question: is Adyen a “fully” digital firm?

IT

Information technology

The hardware and software a business uses to reach its goals.

IS

Information system

Connected parts that manage information to support decisions and control, and help with analysis, visuals and new products.

How an information system turns data into information

Input

Raw data: streams of facts, like single checkout scans.

→

Processing

Classify, arrange and calculate.

→

Output

Information: data in a meaningful, useful form, like sales per product and region.

↺

Feedback: output goes back to the right people so they can check it and correct the input.

Around the system: customers, suppliers, regulators, stockholders and competitors.

Remember: computers and software are the tools and materials. The information system is the house you build with them.

Card 5 of 6 · Business example

SuperMart: from checkout data to strategy

A retail company's business information value chain. Every stage has a business side and a tech side.

Stage

What SuperMart does

Tech that helps

1

Data collection & storage

Collects point-of-sale data from every store: products, amounts, timestamps and customer details from the loyalty program.

IoT devices and sensors · cloud storage · big data technologies · automated data collection

2

Transformation into business systems

Cleans, checks and organizes the raw data into structured databases and data warehouses, ready for analysis.

ETL tools · machine learning to spot patterns and anomalies · data integration platforms · data warehouses and data lakes

3

Dissemination

Makes the processed data available to every department and system.

Enterprise data management · APIs · dashboards and visualization · mobile access anytime, anywhere

4

Business processes

Supply chain: predict demand, optimize stock, manage suppliers.

Enterprise: decide store locations, product mix and resources.

Customer: personalize marketing and service.

Knowledge: share insights across the company.

AI demand forecasting (SCM) · ERP systems · AI-powered CRM · collaborative platforms and wikis

5

Management activities

Planning: sales forecasts guide budgets.

Coordinating: marketing and inventory work together.

Controlling: real-time sales vs targets.

Decision making: pricing, product placement, customer segments.

Predictive analytics and scenario modeling · project and collaboration software · real-time monitoring and alerts · advanced analytics and AI

6

Profitability & strategic position

Targeted marketing campaigns, a cheaper supply chain, new products based on customer preferences, and data-driven expansion into new markets.

Business intelligence tools · competitive intelligence platforms · AI strategic planning tools

Card 6 of 6 · Tech zoom-in

Six tools behind the value chain

Each tool with the problem it solves and an example from the slides.

Stage 1 · Collect

Automated data collection

Counting store visitors:

MANUAL

An employee counts customers.

AUTOMATED

A door sensor counts entries.

Gain: more accurate data, staff free to help customers, real-time monitoring.

Stage 2 · Transform

ETL: Extract, Transform, Load

Extract from the sales database, email list and support logs

Transform: remove duplicates, standardize formats, group customers

Load into one unified customer database

Gain: a single source of truth, a 360° customer view, better targeting.

Stage 2 · Transform

Data integration platform

Before: CRM, online shop and inventory were separate data silos with manual transfers.

After: one online order instantly updates stock, triggers a restock if needed and updates the customer's CRM profile.

Gain: real-time sync, one view of customers, sales and stock, automated reports.

Stage 2 · Store

Data warehouse vs data lake

Warehouse

Structured, processed data for specific business needs. Ready for regular reports.

Lake

Raw data of every type in its original format. Ideal for big data analytics and discoveries.

Clarity and focus vs endless possibilities.

Stage 3 · Share

Enterprise data management

Rules and tools that keep data secure, clean, accessible and consistent across the company.

Global retailer: each region kept sales in a different place (a local database, spreadsheets, cloud storage). After: one data platform, standard reports, role-based access and compliance tracking.

Gain: a data-driven global strategy.

Stage 4 · Use

AI demand forecasting

Clothing retailer:

In: past sales, weather forecasts, trends

AI: finds patterns and predicts demand

Out: right stock levels, efficient distribution, lower costs

Right product, right place, right time.
